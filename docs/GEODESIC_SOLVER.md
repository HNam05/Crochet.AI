# Geodesic Solver

## Status

**FUTURE:** this document specifies a research solver. The bootstrap does not implement a Heat Method, level-set extraction, or geodesic CrochetIR compiler.

The claim labels and common outcomes from [`SOLVER_ARCHITECTURE.md`](SOLVER_ARCHITECTURE.md) apply.

## Intended use

The geodesic family targets mostly continuous freeform amigurumi surfaces for which a stable scalar distance field produces useful course-like level sets. It is algorithmically distinct from the analytic radial-profile solver and the local frontier search.

Required preconditions are:

- a finite, oriented, manifold triangle mesh that passed input preflight;
- dimensions in millimetres and a recorded target hash;
- a deterministic seed or seed set selected from `DesignSpec` constraints;
- level sets whose topology changes can be isolated under the declared numerical profile;
- a resolved `MaterialProfile` applicable to the selected stitch family;
- explicit policies for target boundaries, handles, and intentional openings;
- deterministic numerical and search budgets.

Non-manifold connectivity, unresolved self-intersection, unstable orientation, or near-degenerate topology is not silently repaired. The solver returns `NOT_APPLICABLE`, `INVALID_SOLVER_INPUT`, or `NUMERICAL_FAILURE` with diagnostics.

## Mathematical basis and limits

- **ESTABLISHED:** geodesic distance fields and their level sets are standard tools for surface parameterization and region decomposition.
- **ESTABLISHED:** Heat Method-type algorithms can approximate geodesic distance on triangle meshes through sparse linear solves.
- **ESTABLISHED:** changes in level-set connectivity relate to critical structure captured by Morse/Reeb-style topology summaries under suitable regularity assumptions.
- **ENGINEERING DECISION:** these methods are used to propose course topology and sampling, not to prove that the surface is crochet-constructible or physically attainable.
- **HYPOTHESIS:** an independently reconstructed crochet fabric can approximate suitable target regions when courses follow these level sets within calibrated material limits.

## Pipeline

1. Validate solver-specific mesh preconditions without changing authoritative geometry.
2. Select a seed or seed set deterministically.
3. compute an approximate geodesic scalar field `phi` in millimetres;
4. extract, orient, and canonicalize level-set polylines;
5. detect births, deaths, splits, joins, and unstable critical intervals;
6. form a solver-private Construction Graph for the component/branch plan;
7. choose level values and integer sample counts jointly over each regular region;
8. solve ordered parent-child couplings between adjacent courses;
9. compile complete CrochetIR candidates;
10. discard all solver-private target correspondences at the canonical boundary.

The target mesh may guide steps 1 through 8. It is unavailable to the independent forward model that later verifies a compiled candidate.

## Seed selection

Seed selection affects both course orientation and topology. The run configuration therefore declares one of:

- an explicit target landmark ID;
- a canonical extremum under a declared oriented axis;
- an ordered finite list of seed hypotheses generated by a deterministic rule.

Coordinate lexicographic order alone is not rotation invariant and must not masquerade as a geometric canonical rule. When multiple seeds remain symmetry-equivalent, the solver may emit candidates for each within budget and use canonical hashes only as a final deterministic tie-breaker.

## Distance and level-set extraction

The scalar field `phi(v)` assigns each mesh vertex an approximate distance from the selected seed. Implementations must record:

- method and version;
- sparse solver and ordering policy;
- heat-time parameterization or alternative method parameters;
- convergence residuals and iteration counts;
- all degeneracy and contour-extraction tolerances.

A level value `lambda` induces a piecewise-linear contour `phi(x) = lambda`. Each extracted component is classified as linear or cyclic, receives a canonical orientation, and records its length in millimetres.

Course levels are not produced by independently rounding `lambda / p_c`. Candidate level sequences are selected jointly so that course-pitch residuals, critical intervals, endpoints, and branch transitions are explicit.

## Topology-change detection

A regular interval has a stable number and adjacency of contour components. A critical interval may contain a component birth, death, split, join, or handle-related event.

The solver constructs a topology event only when it is stable under the declared critical-value and contour-snap tolerances. Near-equal critical values, plateaus, or mesh-dependent component flicker produce `TOPOLOGY_UNSTABLE`; they are not resolved by arbitrary perturbation unless the perturbation policy is explicit, deterministic, bounded, and recorded.

At a stable event:

- regular components become Construction Graph regions;
- split/join events create frontier obligations;
- a birth must have an explicit construction anchor or attach policy;
- a death must close or intentionally terminate its frontier;
- a handle may require a join, opening, or seam alternative.

The resulting Construction Graph is a plan, not CrochetIR and not proof of feasibility. See [`TOPOLOGY_SEAMLESS.md`](TOPOLOGY_SEAMLESS.md).

## Course sampling

Let a regular contour have length `L_i` and let `p_s` be `effective_stitch_pitch_mm`. Its real nominal count is `L_i / p_s`, but the integer count is chosen jointly with neighboring contours and construction constraints.

For each selected count `n_i`, sample positions are ordered by contour arc length. A cyclic contour also has a phase hypothesis. Sample locations and target-normal information are solver-private proposal data; canonical CrochetIR retains construction relationships rather than target coordinates as truth.

The count search must account globally for:

- course length residuals;
- exact reachable transitions;
- topology events and branch obligations;
- allowed shaping density;
- angular/arc-length stacking history;
- finite count and phase hypothesis windows.

## Adjacent-course coupling

For two adjacent regular contours, coupling is an ordered partition problem. Partition the current ordered base indices and the next ordered top indices into matched consecutive groups. Each group has an allowed `(base_arity, top_arity)`:

```text
(1, 1) ordinary stitch
(1, 2) SC-style increase
(2, 1) SC-style decrease
```

Required invariants are:

- every base index appears in exactly one group;
- every top index appears in exactly one group;
- group order is monotone, modulo one recorded phase cut for cyclic courses;
- no correspondence crosses another in the unwrapped order;
- arities use enabled canonical operations only;
- unsupported multiplicity is rejected rather than decomposed ambiguously;
- target-distance cost can rank couplings but cannot waive any invariant.

**PROVEN / FORMAL:** a complete ordered partition by supported arities supplies exact base/top accounting for that course transition.

At a topology split or join, the solver does not force multiple contour components through this pairwise model. It emits an explicit Construction Graph event and later compiles explicit CrochetIR frontier operations.

## Candidate objective

Within a fixed regular region, candidate generation uses a versioned lexicographic cost such as:

```text
(
  maximum_contour_length_residual_mm,
  sum_contour_length_residual_squared_mm2,
  coupling_distortion_mm2,
  shaping_stack_penalty,
  phase_change_penalty_rad,
  canonical_coupling_trace
)
```

This target-aware cost is permitted only in the solver. It is not reused by the independent simulator and it is not a geometry verification score.

## Deterministic budgets

All fields are required positive integers in the run configuration:

| Parameter | Unit | Bound |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `max_seed_hypotheses` | seeds | Distance fields attempted |
| `max_sparse_iterations_per_solve` | iterations | Each numerical linear solve |
| `max_level_sequences` | sequences | Joint course-level hypotheses |
| `max_contour_components` | components | Extracted components retained across a sequence |
| `max_count_hypotheses_per_contour` | integer counts | Count states per contour |
| `max_phase_hypotheses_per_cyclic_course` | phases | Cyclic unwrappings evaluated |
| `max_coupling_states` | ordered partition states | Adjacent-course coupling search |
| `max_topology_backtracks` | branch-plan alternatives | Construction Graph exploration |
| `max_emitted_candidates` | CrochetIR artifacts | Complete candidates compiled |

Sparse-solver non-convergence returns `NUMERICAL_FAILURE`. Exhausting a combinatorial work counter returns `SEARCH_BUDGET_EXHAUSTED`. Both preserve partial diagnostics but emit no partial CrochetIR.

## Numerical tolerances

Values are mandatory in a versioned numerical profile and have no implicit bootstrap default.

| Tolerance | Unit | Purpose and rationale | Owner / validation path |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `degenerate_face_area_mm2` | mm^2 | Rejects triangles too small for stable gradients | Geometry preflight / scale-covariance and remeshing fixtures |
| `linear_solve_relative_residual` | dimensionless | Required residual for each sparse solve | Geodesic solver / convergence studies on analytic meshes |
| `level_set_vertex_snap_mm` | mm | Resolves contours passing numerically near a vertex | Contour extractor / retessellation metamorphic tests |
| `critical_value_separation_mm` | mm | Distinguishes isolated topology events from degenerate/near-simultaneous events | Topology engine / Y-branch, torus, and two-lobe fixtures |
| `component_length_floor_mm` | mm | Rejects contour fragments below numerically resolvable scale | Geodesic solver / mesh-noise adversarial fixtures |
| `coupling_cost_tie_tolerance_mm2` | mm^2 | Defines deterministic equality for coupling costs | Coupling solver / rigid-motion and canonicalization tests |

These are algorithmic tolerances. Hard target-shape thresholds are owned by verification.

## Complexity

Let `V`, `E`, and `F` be mesh sizes; `M` selected level sets; `W` retained count/phase states; and `P` coupling states per transition.

- Distance computation is dominated by sparse factorization/solves; the exact cost depends on matrix ordering and conditioning and must be measured, not hidden behind a linear-time claim.
- Contour extraction is at most `O(M * F)` for a direct scan and may be reduced by an indexed implementation.
- Coupling is `O(M * P)` under the configured state bound.
- Construction Graph exploration is bounded by `max_topology_backtracks`.

Memory includes the mesh, scalar field, sparse factors, contour geometry, and bounded coupling states. Implementations must publish observed peak memory for benchmark size classes.

## Failure modes

- `NOT_APPLICABLE`: non-manifold mesh, unsupported topology, unstable seed policy, or level sets unsuitable for courses.
- `TOPOLOGY_UNSTABLE` diagnostic under `NUMERICAL_FAILURE`: degenerate or tolerance-sensitive critical structure.
- `NO_FEASIBLE_CONSTRUCTION`: a fully enumerated declared coupling/topology domain has no exact supported construction.
- `SEARCH_BUDGET_EXHAUSTED`: incomplete seed, level, count, coupling, or branch-plan search.
- `NUMERICAL_FAILURE`: ill-conditioned or unconverged distance/contour computation.
- Candidate rejection: invalid frontier plan, unreachable arity transition, self-crossing ordered coupling, or unaccounted component.

## Known limitations and counterexamples

- A torus can produce level-set splits/joins that a single uninterrupted cyclic course sequence cannot represent without explicit topology operations.
- A nearly flat scalar-field plateau can change extracted connectivity under tiny mesh perturbations; arbitrary tie-breaking would create false topology certainty.
- Two thin nearby branches can merge numerically before a physically usable join exists.
- A valid noncrossing target correspondence can still produce a physically colliding crochet object.
- Retessellation can perturb approximate geodesics and critical values. Robustness to reasonable retessellation is a required metamorphic test, not assumed.
